# TradingCore dry run — findings (2026-09-16 10:20 EDT)

Run started 2026-09-16 08:37 EDT (Gemma 4 12B + MTP, thinking off, dry run on live OKX ETH-USDT-SWAP, $10 sim).
Data: `samples.jsonl` (5-min collector), bot logs, `candles_7d.json` (7.6 days of 1m candles for backtests).

## 1. Mechanically the bot works
100+ minutes: one websocket drop (09:18) reconnected and re-synced; feed age < 0.1 s; verdicts ~550 ms median
(2 ms prompt-eval on repeats = prompt cache working); every gate rejects for the reason on its label; stops/targets
fill at the right prices; statistics correct; GPU guards refuse everything else. Zero errors.

## 2. Direction
All trades were SHORT because ETH sat below its 100-minute EMA for every minute of the run; Scalpel is trend-following
and only goes LONG above the EMA. In the 7.6-day backtest the rule produced 571 longs / 621 shorts. Not a bug.

## 3. The Scalpel entry rule has NO edge (this is the headline)
Backtest of the exact rule (`backtest_scalpel.py`, decisions at closes, conservative exits, 0.16% round-trip fee),
1,192 trades over 7.6 days:

| variant | trades | win % (pre-fee) | gross %/trade | net %/trade |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| live settings (band 0.25, SL 1×, TP 1.5×, 5 candles) | 1192 | 45 | +0.006 | −0.154 |
| contrarian (same entries, opposite side) | 1189 | 44 | −0.005 | — |
| no EMA gate | 1572 | 46 | +0.005 | — |
| random entries (20 seeds) | ~1200 | — | +0.001 (±0.005) | — |
| best of 300 band/stop/target/horizon variants | 284 | 41 | +0.018 | −0.142 |

Scalpel's gross is inside the noise band of random entry; no parameter combination is net positive. Tuning cannot fix it.

## 4. The model layer adds no selectivity
Every verdict of the run scored on what price did next (bot exits, gross): Executes n=23 → +0.047% / 57% wins;
Aborts n=2,445 → +0.053% / 65% wins. The model's yes/no is not better than its no. (All were shorts in a downtrend,
hence the positive gross on both sides.)

## 5. Fees vs targets
Median target 0.167% of price, median stop 0.116%, round-trip fee 0.160% → a target hit nets +0.007%, a stop costs
−0.276%; break-even win rate 97%. Live sim after 23 trades: gross +$1.04, fees −$5.07.

$2.59; slope calls direction 48%. → replaced by a realized-volatility band; slope weight 0 in the reversal score

## 9. Learning from its own trades — attempted, NOT working yet (2026-09-16 ~11:40 EDT)
`dryrun/learn.py` builds a labelled dataset from the bot's own post-signal cycles (features = every filter's
score/flag + packet scores + wall zone + divergence side; label = did the setup win after the 0.16% fee, judged
by what price actually did next with the bot's exits), fits a logistic regression (numpy, no sklearn) and
evaluates **strictly out of time** (train on the earlier 60%, test on the later 40%).

Result on 4,121 labelled setups (train 12:37–14:11 UTC, test 14:11–15:25 UTC):

| | |
|---|---|
| out-of-time AUC | 0.698 (0.5 = no skill) |
| base win rate after fees | train 25.9% → **test 9.0%** |
| take everything, test | −0.176% net per setup |
| gate p≥0.3 (takes 13%) | −0.252% net — **worse than taking everything** |
| gate p≥0.5 (takes 3%) | −0.298% net — worse still |

**Read it as a negative result.** Ranking skill exists (AUC 0.70) but does not survive contact with the fee, and
the strongest feature is `minute_of_hour` (+1.61) — a regime artefact, not a market truth. The base rate
collapsing 26% → 9% between the two halves says the label distribution is non-stationary over 90 minutes.
Do not wire this into the bot. It needs: days of data (not hours), features that are not filter outputs of a
zero-edge signal, and a walk-forward evaluation across several sessions.

The machinery is right and reusable — labelled dataset, out-of-time split, AUC, gate sweep — so when a finder
with real edge exists, this measures it honestly in seconds.
