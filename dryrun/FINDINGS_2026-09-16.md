## 9. Learning from its own trades — attempted, NOT working yet (2026-09-16 ~11:40 EDT)
`dryrun/learn.py` builds a labelled dataset from the bot's own post-signal cycles (features = every filter's
score/flag + packet scores + wall zone + divergence side; label = did the setup win after the 0.16% fee, judged
by what price actually did next with the bot's exits), fits a logistic regression (numpy, no sklearn) and
evaluates **strictly out of time** (train on the earlier 60%, test on the later 40%).

Result on 4,121 labelled setups (train 12:37–14:11 UTC, test 14:11–15:25 UTC):

| | |
|---|---|
| out-of-time AUC | 0.698 (0.5 = no skill) |
| base win rate after fees | train 25.9% → **test 9.0%** |
| take everything, test | −0.176% net per setup |
| gate p≥0.3 (takes 13%) | −0.252% net — **worse than taking everything** |
| gate p≥0.5 (takes 3%) | −0.298% net — worse still |

**Read it as a negative result.** Ranking skill exists (AUC 0.70) but does not survive contact with the fee, and
the strongest feature is `minute_of_hour` (+1.61) — a regime artefact, not a market truth. The base rate
collapsing 26% → 9% between the two halves says the label distribution is non-stationary over 90 minutes.
Do not wire this into the bot. It needs: days of data (not hours), features that are not filter outputs of a
zero-edge signal, and a walk-forward evaluation across several sessions.

The machinery is right and reusable — labelled dataset, out-of-time split, AUC, gate sweep — so when a finder
with real edge exists, this measures it honestly in seconds.

## 10. The all-SHORT mystery — SOLVED (2026-09-16 ~11:40 EDT, commit 769c304)
Every trade of the first two runs was a SHORT. Two independent causes, both measured over 7.7 days of candles:

**(a) Scalpel's EMA100 trend gate.** Over a week it is *not* short-biased (571 LONG / 629 SHORT) — but regimes
are long: the median run is 3 minutes yet the max is 238, so a 2–4 h session sits inside ONE regime.
The first live run had price below the EMA for **169 of its 174 minutes**, so only shorts were reachable.

| Scalpel variant | trades | LONG | SHORT | gross %/trade |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| EMA gate (old) | 1200 | 571 | 629 | +0.0060 |
| **no gate (now)** | **1581** | **784** | **797** | **+0.0050** |
| fade instead of momentum | 1576 | 783 | 793 | −0.0024 |

The gate bought **nothing** (both inside the ±0.005% random-entry noise band) and cost 32% of the trades plus
half the opportunities in any session. Removed; `SCALPEL_REQUIRE_TREND=true` restores it.

**(b) TrapX's `if/elif` tie-break — a genuine structural short bias.** 7.6% of candles have BOTH wicks beating
1.5× the body, and the `elif` handed every one to SHORT: **63% short, vs 49% when the tie goes to the bigger
wick.** Worse in practice: the live candle's body is ~0 for the first seconds of every minute, which is exactly
when TrapX evaluates it. Fixed: the bigger wick wins, equal wicks produce no signal, and a wick must also be
≥ `TRAPX_WICK_MIN_RANGE_FRACTION` (30%) of the candle's range so a zero body can no longer manufacture one.

**Live result within 8 minutes of deploying:** 173 LONG / 231 SHORT signals (43% long) in a market still
drifting down, and the first LONG trade of the whole project executed. Direction now follows price action
instead of a hardcoded regime filter.

## 11. Restart orphaned the open position (found live, fixed — commits 3b46a98 + 77b8a4f)
The scoreboard reported "1 open" while the bot was flat. Cause: the Rolling5 lifecycle lives in memory but the
position record lives in `simulation_state.json`. A restart dropped the lifecycle, the engine believed it was
flat, and the next trade **overwrote** the stored position — fee already charged, outcome never realised.
Evidence: the SHORT opened 15:25:49 UTC was orphaned by the 15:30 restart.

Fixed: `Engine.resume_open_position()` re-attaches the lifecycle at start-up, `_open_simulated_position` refuses
to overwrite an open position, and `sim_stats` reports `orphaned_opens` instead of folding them into `open_now`.
Note for live trading: the same class of bug exists there (`open_position` is in memory), and the real fix is
querying the exchange for open positions at start-up — not wired, because no exchange is chosen yet.

## 12. THE BINDING CONSTRAINT: the target was smaller than the fee (commit 5ffdadb)
Measured on the live trades, not theory:

| | |
|---|---|
| median stop distance | 0.099% of price |
| median target distance | 0.140% of price |
| round-trip taker fee | **0.160% of notional** |
| net on a trade that hits its target exactly | **−0.020%** |
| break-even win rate | **108% — impossible** |
| fee per round trip, as a share of posted margin | **32%** |

Every trade was a guaranteed loser regardless of direction accuracy. Two fixes:
- `MIN_REWARD_FEE_MULTIPLE` (3×) refuses any signal whose target cannot clear the round-trip fee, before a model
  call is spent on it. This is a permanent safety gate, not a tuning knob.
- Scalpel's stop/target were hardcoded 1.0× / 1.5× the level candle's range → now
  `SCALPEL_STOP_RANGE_MULTIPLE` (3.0) and `SCALPEL_TARGET_RANGE_MULTIPLE` (8.0), target ≈ 3.3× the fee.

**And the honest headline.** Re-running the grid with the trend gate off and wide targets: the best configuration
(stop 6×, target 12×, 30-candle horizon) reaches gross **+0.039%** per trade — the first result meaningfully
outside the ±0.005% random-entry noise band, i.e. a real if tiny directional edge. But:

> **0 of the 50 configurations whose target clears the fee 3× are net positive at a 0.16% taker fee.**

The fee is the binding constraint, not the signal. At maker rates (~0.02% a side = 0.04% round trip) that same
+0.039% gross becomes roughly break-even, and at a rebate it is positive. **Limit-order execution is not an
optimisation — it is the difference between a system that cannot work and one that might.** That is the next
build: pending order with a fill window, maker on entry and target, taker on stops. The backtest model for it
already exists in `backtest_scalpel.py` (`run_limit`).

## 13. Wider targets + 200x leverage were incompatible — sizing was the missing piece (commit 5a5232e)
The first LONG taken under the new wide targets lost **12.9% of the account in one trade**. Not an exit bug —
arithmetic:

| | |
|---|---|
| stop distance | 0.487% of price |
| **liquidation distance at 200x** | **0.500% of price** |
| → the stop sat at | **97% of the way to liquidation** |
| loss at the stop | 97% of the posted margin |
| with fees | 12.9% of the account |

**The trap.** To clear a 0.16% round-trip fee 3×, a target must be ≥0.48%. A stop proportionate to that target is
0.2–0.5%. But at 200× leverage liquidation is at 0.50%. *Any stop wide enough to matter sits at the liquidation
point.* Fee pressure pushes stops out; leverage forbids it.

**Why it hurt so much: sizing fixed the BET, not the LOSS.** Margin was a fixed 10% of the account, so the loss
per trade scaled with stop width:

| stop | loss under fixed-10% margin |
|---|---|
| 0.10% | 2.0% of account |
| 0.25% | 5.0% |
| 0.487% | 9.7% |
| 0.50% | 10.0% (the whole margin) |

**Fixed:** sizing is now risk-based. The position is chosen so that hitting the stop costs
`RISK_PER_TRADE_PERCENT` (2%) of the account; `RISK_CAP_PERCENT` (10%, the operator's rule) becomes a **ceiling
on the margin** rather than a fixed bet. A wide stop now buys a smaller position instead of a bigger loss.
Replaying the real trade: same entry, same stop, **2.0% of the account instead of 12.9%**, notional 4× smaller.

Note this does not change the *edge* — it changes survivability. At the measured 28% win rate, losing 10% of the
account per trade is ruin in a handful of trades; losing 2% is survivable long enough to measure whether an edge
exists at all.

## 14. "It shouldn't open unless it can walk away with a scalp" — why it can't do that
Two separate requirements in that sentence. Both measured; both currently impossible.

### (a) "Only open if the next few candles are favourable" requires predicting them. Nothing does.
10,859 samples, every cheap price-derived feature, accuracy at calling the next 6 candles' direction:

| feature | accuracy |
|---|---|
| 10-close regression slope | 49.3% |
| 3-candle momentum | 49.0% |
| price vs EMA100 | 48.5% |
| **mean reversion (best)** | **51.6%** |
| coin flip | 50.0% |

The system does not open trades *because it knows* the next candles are favourable. It opens them because a
geometric rule fired. It has no way to know, and neither does any feature tested.

### (b) A "scalp" has a minimum size set by fees, and that size is not a scalp at taker rates.
Max favourable move over the next N candles (10,969 samples):

| horizon | median | reaches 0.48% (3× taker fee) | reaches 0.12% (3× maker fee) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 candles | 0.058% | **1.6%** of the time | 24.8% |
| 15 candles | 0.106% | 5.6% | 45.9% |
| 30 candles | 0.150% | 11.8% | 57.8% |

A 0.48% move (the minimum that pays at taker fees) takes a **median 46 minutes** and only arrives at all 36% of
the time inside two hours. That is a swing trade, not a scalp. A 0.12% move — viable at maker fees — takes a
median **11 minutes**. **Maker fees are what make scalping physically possible.**

### (c) Even with maker fees and small fast targets: 0 of 100 configurations net positive.
Stops cost the taker fee *plus* the stop distance while wins earn only the target *minus* maker fees, so the
break-even win rate stays far above what a coin-flip entry delivers. Example: TP 0.12% / SL 0.15% wins 47% of
the time and still loses 0.11% per trade (break-even needs 76%).

### (d) Even the one >50% signal loses: mean-reversion entries, 0 of 243 configurations net positive.
Some reach a **71.4% win rate** and still lose money — wins of 0.12% against losses of 0.40%.

### THE EQUATION THAT GOVERNS EVERYTHING
> **net per trade = gross edge − fees**
> best gross edge ever measured here: **+0.039%** · maker fees **0.04%** · taker fees **0.16%**
> → +0.039 − 0.04 = **−0.001%** (break-even at best, with perfect maker fills)
> → +0.039 − 0.16 = **−0.121%** (what we actually run)

No target, stop, horizon, trailing rule or exit logic changes this. **Only a bigger gross edge does.** Stop
tuning exits; the entry is the whole problem.

### The one avenue not yet exhausted
Every feature tested above is derived from *price*. The order book is not, and until today the bot could only
see 5 levels of it. It now merges the full book and tracks whale-sized resting liquidity — which is exactly
TrapX's original thesis (spot a big player building a trap, ride it). That is the only remaining place a
gross edge above 0.16% might live, and it is now measurable for the first time.

## 15. 200x leverage is now provably the binding constraint (commits 3734d49, c744068)
Two guards were added after live evidence, and together they make the leverage cost arithmetic rather than opinion.

**Guard 1 — stop too tight for fees.** Under risk-based sizing the position is *risk ÷ stop distance*, so a tight
stop buys a large position and the fee scales with it:
> fee as a fraction of the amount risked = **fee% ÷ stop%**

Live stops of 0.047–0.118% against a 0.160% fee meant paying **1.3×–3.4× the amount risked** in fees. Over 12
trades: gross −15.25, fees −26.10. `MIN_STOP_FEE_MULTIPLE` (2.0) now refuses them → **stop floor 0.32%**.

**Guard 2 — stop beyond liquidation.** A live SHORT had its stop 4.670% away while liquidation at 200× is at
0.500%: the stop was 9.3× beyond reach, so the stated risk/reward was fiction and the real risk was the whole
margin. `MAX_STOP_LIQUIDATION_FRACTION` (0.8) now refuses them → **stop ceiling 0.40% at 200×**.

**The window that leaves:**

| leverage | workable stop band | % of ETH 1m candles that fit |
|---|---|---|
| **200× (current)** | **0.32% – 0.40%** | **7%** |
| 100× | 0.32% – 0.80% | 17% |
| 50× | 0.32% – 1.60% | 19% |
| 25× | 0.32% – 3.20% | 20% |

Observed immediately after deploying: **57 of 60 rejections were STOP BEYOND LIQUIDATION.** The bot is now
refusing almost every signal it generates — correctly, because at 200× almost none of them is a real trade.

**Leverage is now free to lower.** Since sizing became risk-based (commit 5a5232e) the position is determined by
risk ÷ stop, and leverage only decides how much margin is posted and where liquidation sits. It no longer affects
position size, edge, or fees. At 200× it buys nothing and costs ~93% of the usable stop range.
Config also now refuses to start if the fee floor exceeds the liquidation ceiling, naming the three ways out.

## 2026-09-16 17:14 EDT — the order book carries information; price does not

Every price-derived test in this document is a negative result. This one is not.

`analyse_live_walls.py` over **1,981 wall endings** recorded live across three detector settings running
concurrently. The naive test — "what does price do after a wall ends?" — is worthless, because if the
market drifted during the sample then everything looks directional. The only wall-specific measure is the
**difference between what follows a bid-wall ending and what follows an ask-wall ending**. Half of that
difference is what one leg of a trade could earn, and it has to beat the fee to mean anything.

| setting | horizon | n | after bid | after ask | wall signal | t | per side |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 50x/0.1%/20s | 5 min | 1388 | −0.0223 | −0.0744 | +0.0521 | **+7.6** | +0.0260 |
| 50x/0.1%/20s | 10 min | 1388 | −0.1085 | −0.1423 | +0.0338 | +6.3 | +0.0169 |
| 50x/0.1%/20s | 20 min | 1388 | −0.1446 | −0.1997 | +0.0551 | +6.7 | +0.0275 |
| 100x/0.1%/30s | 5 min | 451 | −0.0295 | −0.0915 | +0.0620 | +5.3 | +0.0310 |
| 100x/0.1%/30s | 20 min | 451 | −0.1440 | −0.2124 | +0.0684 | +4.3 | +0.0342 |
| 200x/0.1%/45s | 20 min | 109 | −0.1435 | −0.2446 | +0.1011 | +2.8 | +0.0506 ← clears maker |

**An ask wall ending is followed by price falling further than a bid wall ending.** The sign is consistent
across every setting and every horizon past one minute, and the t-statistics are large. This is TrapX's
original premise — whales building size and pulling it — showing measurable information for the first time
in this project.

What this does **not** yet establish:
- The sample is 2.3 hours of a single afternoon, and the market drifted down throughout it.
- Only the 200x setting at a 20-minute horizon clears the maker fee, and that cell has n=109.
- A 20-minute horizon is not a scalp. The 5-minute cells are the scalp-shaped ones and they earn about
  +0.026% to +0.031% per side against a 0.04% maker round trip.

So the next step is **more data and a calibrated classifier**, not a module rewrite.

### The classifier needs calibrating before any of this can be traded
`walls.jsonl` holds 51,253 wall endings over 2h17m, classified **51,250 "pulled" / 3 "faded" / 0 "absorbed"**.
Zero absorptions is not a market fact, it is a threshold that is catching ordinary book churn rather than
resting whale size. "Pulled" is supposed to be the trap springing — size vanishing at a price the market
never reached — and it cannot mean that if it describes everything. Fix the calibration before trading it.
